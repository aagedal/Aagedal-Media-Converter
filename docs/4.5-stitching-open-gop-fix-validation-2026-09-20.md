# 4.5 open-GOP stitching fix — 2026-09-20

## Change

Trimmed merge preparation now removes leading video packets whose presentation
timestamp precedes the first copied video packet. An open GOP may contain these
pictures after its first keyframe in decode order; they depend on pictures from
the discarded GOP. Concatenating them after another clip allowed the decoder to
use that clip's reference pictures, producing two frames that matched neither
source in the retained H.264 regression.

The filter runs only when preparation seeks past the source start. It uses the
bundled FFmpeg's `noise` bitstream filter with explicit `amount=0` and
`drop='not(eq(pts,nopts))*lt(pts,startpts)'`. Packet payloads are unchanged,
missing presentation timestamps are excluded from the drop condition, later
B-frame ordering remains intact, and audio is not filtered. No new subprocess,
probe, dependency, grant, or execution owner is introduced.

The expression uses the first timestamp actually copied, rather than the user's
requested cut: Stream Copy can seek to an earlier keyframe. FFmpeg documents
[`startpts`, `nopts`, and deterministic packet dropping](https://www.ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-bitstream-filters.html#noise).
A preliminary bundled-FFmpeg experiment reproduced the defect with every tested
negative-timestamp mode; changing timestamp normalization alone did not fix it.

## Regression expectations

The previous two strict expected failures are now ordinary passing assertions.
For generated 24 fps open-GOP H.264 with fixed B-frames and stereo AAC, retaining
0.5–1.5 seconds from each source now produces 52 reported and decoded frames.
Every decoded frame must byte-match the independently decoded source selection:
frames 12 through 36 plus frame 39 from each original, in sequence order.
There are no mismatching extra frames at the join.

Two additional cases request 0.625–1.5 seconds, starting between keyframes,
with marker export enabled and disabled. They require the same source-frame
identity because seeking still starts at the preceding keyframe. All four cases
also check stereo tone order and continuity, chapter/EDL markers when enabled,
and successful serialization with a waiting agent conversion.

This fixes the demonstrated leading-picture corruption, not exact trimming.
Reference-frame tails and audio preroll still extend the requested boundaries;
the existing Stream Copy notice remains applicable. These generated fixtures do
not establish universal open-GOP safety for every codec, encoder, or container.
Broader real camera/MXF/long-recording playback and export remain release work.
