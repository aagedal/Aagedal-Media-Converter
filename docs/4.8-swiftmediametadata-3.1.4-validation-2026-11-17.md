# 4.8 SwiftMediaMetadata 3.1.4 update — 2026-11-17

The app now pins the published SwiftMediaMetadata 3.1.4 release at revision
`db85eb6934e31b4a33824d92445b541517fd2014` ([PR #7](https://github.com/aagedal/SwiftMediaMetadata/pull/7)).

## The bug

A YouTube live stream recorded as a growing file with 4.8.0 beta 1 showed its
growing length while recording, then about one second after Stop. yt-dlp-rs
1.0.0-rc.2 no longer rewrites the finished recording, so it stays a fragmented
MOV (ffmpeg `frag_keyframe`, with an `mfra` but no `mehd`). Its `moov` describes
only the first fragment: `mvhd` 1.02 s, video `mdhd` 1.0 s with 30 samples. The
other 319 seconds are in `moof` boxes. AVFoundation and ffprobe add the
fragments and read 320 s; SwiftMediaMetadata 3.1.3 read only `moov`, so
`SwiftExifMediaProbe.duration` and the queue item's details reported 1.02 s.
Up to rc.1 the `--embed-metadata` rewrite produced a plain MOV, which hid this.

## The fix

3.1.4 reads `moov > mvex` (`mehd`, `trex`) and every top-level `moof > traf >
trun`, and adds each track's fragment samples (with `tfhd`/`trex` defaults) to
its duration, frame count and byte total. `mehd`, when present, sets the movie
duration; otherwise the longest audio/video track does, as in ffprobe.

On the 320-second recording, 3.1.4 matches ffprobe exactly: format 320.017 s,
video 319.967 s / 9,599 frames / 1,006,285 b/s, audio 320.017 s / 128,008 b/s.
FFmpeg `frag_keyframe`, `frag_keyframe+empty_moov`, `+default_base_moof` and
`empty_moov+separate_moof` test files match ffprobe's stream durations and frame
counts, and its format duration within 12 ms. Plain files are unchanged.
