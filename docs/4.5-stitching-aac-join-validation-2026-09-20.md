# 4.5 AAC stitching join validation — 2026-09-20

## Scope

Two live regressions exercise `ConversionManager.convertGroup` with bundled
FFmpeg, all-intra H.264 video and mono 48 kHz AAC audio. Each two-second source
is trimmed to 0.5–1.5 seconds; distinct 440 Hz and 880 Hz tones identify the two
clips. One case disables stitch markers and the other enables them, covering
the explicit concat-duration path used by marker export.

Both cases verify:

- Both queue rows complete and point to the same merged output.
- An independently submitted agent job waits until merge publication, then
  succeeds with its own output.
- Merged video retains 48 frames; both outputs decode without FFmpeg errors.
- Audio retains one channel at 48 kHz, and output duration stays within four
  1024-sample packets of the expected two seconds.
- Decoded merged audio contains 96,000–100,096 samples. Interior tone checks
  identify both clips in order, and every tested 10 ms window across the join
  has RMS amplitude above 1,000 on the signed 16-bit scale.
- Marker-enabled output has both cut chapters and EDL labels. Its second
  chapter starts within two AAC packets of one second, and the final chapter
  ends at the measured output duration within one millisecond.

## Limits

These generated, matching-format sources establish bounded packet-level behavior,
not sample-exact cuts or perceptually seamless joins for arbitrary AAC media.
The tests do not establish stereo/multichannel joins, differing encoder delay,
long-GOP joins, mixed source formats, or real camera-card/MXF/long-recording
behavior. Editor-driven export and OS-level grant revocation remain open.

No production code, dependencies, or bundled binaries changed. This continuation
does not claim a full unit-suite run, Release package, or signing/notarization.
